Figure 1 captures the general conceptual model of the Public Services Ontology.

The ontology provides a conceptual model for describing public services and administrative procedures offered by local administrations. It is intended to support the harmonised publication of information about municipal services, procedures, requirements, documentation, responsible organisations and administrative interactions.

The model reuses and extends the Core Public Service Vocabulary (CPSV), a vocabulary designed to describe public services in a structured and interoperable way. Following the CPSV pattern, the ontology represents a public service as an activity or function provided by a public organisation in response to the needs of citizens, businesses or other agents. Administrative procedures are represented as formal processes associated with the access, request, management or resolution of those services.

The ontology also reuses elements from the Vocabulary for the representation of services and administrative procedures offered by a municipality, which also imports CPSV and was developed to describe municipal services and administrative procedures. In particular, this ontology reuses the class DestinadoProcedimiento to represent the target entity of a Public Service.

The main conceptual elements of the ontology include:
- Public Service: represents a service provided by a public administration, such as waste collection, citizen assistance, urban planning services or social services.
- Administrative Procedure: represents a formal administrative process through which a person or organisation interacts with the administration, for example to submit an application, request a licence, register information, pay a fee, obtain a certificate or lodge a complaint.
- Public Organisation: represents the public body, department or administrative unit responsible for providing a service or managing a procedure.
- Input: represents the information, forms or documents that must be provided to initiate or complete a procedure.
- Output: represents the result produced by a procedure, such as an authorisation, licence, certificate, resolution or registration.
- Rule: represents the legal, regulatory or administrative framework that governs the provision of a service or the execution of a procedure.

In addition to these core classes, the ontology can be complemented with controlled vocabularies for classifying public service types and administrative procedure types. These controlled vocabularies make it possible to distinguish between the domain of the service, such as public safety, waste management, water supply or urban maintenance, and the type of administrative interaction, such as application, communication, responsible declaration, authorisation, registration, payment, complaint or appeal.
